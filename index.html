Arkansas snapshot

Arkansas Infant & Child Death Review reporting for deaths occurring in 2023 documents 12 unintentional drowning deaths among infants and children. Most occurred in open water, with a smaller — but operationally important — fraction in pool-or-bathtub settings.

Why bathtubs still matter — even when open water dominates

Infants: bathtub concentration

National NCHS surveillance reports that during 2018–2019, most unintentional drowning deaths among infants under age 1 occurred in bathtubs — about 75%.

Source: NCHS Data Brief 413

In-home drowning is often a bathtub event

CPSC reports nearly 90 children drown inside the home each year, two-thirds of them in the bathtub. A child can drown in as little as 2 inches of water — quickly, and silently.

Source: CPSC In-Home Drowning Safety Information Center

Our intervention

The Continuous-Contact Bath Safety System

CCBSS is a combined behavioral and wearable approach designed to reduce risk during the predictable interruptions that bath time always brings.

Touch-First Bath Protocol

1
Stage

Before the water is turned on, stage towels, diaper, clothes, and soap within arm’s reach.

2
Touch

Maintain continuous physical contact the entire time baby is in the tub.

3
Interruptions

Extract → Secure → Resolve. Remove baby from the water first. Every time.

Design evidence: Johnson et al. (2025)

Wearable concept

Seraph Safety Wrap

A rapid-securing, high-friction wrap intended to help caregivers stabilize a wet, slippery child after extraction — not for in-water use.

Important: any wearable that supports infant weight must be designed and tested to applicable carrier safety standards.

16 CFR Part 1226 · CPSC carrier guidance
